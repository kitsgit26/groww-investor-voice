Groww Investor Voice – Qualitative Survey Analysis
1. Research Overview

Research method: Open-ended questionnaire responses
Platform: Google Forms
Collection date: 9 November 2026
Total responses: 10
Eligible first-time investors: 6

This analysis examines written responses to understand the experiences, concerns and unmet needs of first-time mutual fund SIP investors.

These are qualitative survey findings, not interview findings.

2. Emerging Themes
Theme 1: Difficulty managing monthly SIP deductions

Respondents described challenges involving automatic deductions, monthly commitments and SIP management.

Evidence:

One respondent described difficulty controlling investment amounts and requested confirmation before deductions.
Another described an SIP becoming difficult to maintain because of monthly financial commitments and failed automatic payments.

Potential user need: Clearer deduction information and greater control over SIP management.

Theme 2: Difficulty understanding investment performance

Some respondents described confusion when their investment values changed or markets fluctuated.

Evidence:

A respondent reported checking investments daily and feeling worried about negative returns.
Another requested simpler explanations of market fluctuations.

Potential user need: Contextual explanations of investment performance in beginner-friendly language.

Theme 3: Lack of beginner-friendly explanations

Respondents mentioned difficulty understanding NAV, mutual fund terminology and investment categories.

Evidence:

One respondent requested simple language and practical examples.
Another described difficulty understanding equity, debt and hybrid mutual funds.

Potential user need: Accessible educational explanations within the investment experience.

Theme 4: Dependence on external guidance

Respondents reported using friends, family, YouTube, Google and online communities to resolve questions.

Potential user need: Reliable, accessible explanations within the investment platform.

Theme 5: Need for post-investment guidance

One respondent described wanting regular guidance about what to expect after starting an SIP.

Potential user need: Ongoing educational support beyond the initial investment process.

3. Behavioural Observations
Some respondents checked their investments daily.
Others checked mainly after SIP deductions.
Participants used multiple external sources to understand investment-related questions.
Financial commitments appeared in responses about difficulty maintaining SIPs.
Some respondents reported continuing despite uncertainty.

These observations describe this small sample and should not be generalised to all Groww users.

4. Potential Product Opportunities
Clearer SIP deduction and transaction tracking.
Contextual explanations of investment performance.
Simple explanations of investment terminology.
Accessible post-investment educational support.
Clear SIP management information.

These are opportunities for further validation, not final solution decisions.

5. Research Limitations
Only six respondents met the primary eligibility criteria.
The sample was recruited through convenience sampling.
Written survey responses did not allow follow-up questions.
Some answers were brief or incomplete.
The survey did not establish which platform participants used.
No causal relationship with SIP continuation has been established.
6. Research Integrity

All observations are based on actual survey responses supplied for analysis.

No interviews are claimed to have taken place.

The illustrative R01–R10 dataset is excluded as primary research evidence.
